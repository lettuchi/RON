# Ryoko Owari Nights · Case Zero

### *零番 (the docket before the knock)*

**Content note.** Light explicit tone: coercive magistrate appetite, witness expendability, harsh mercy. No on-page sex. Kaoru POV where marked. Precedes [Prologue · The Wrong Door](./ryoko-owari-nights-novel.md).

---

## SUMMARY ONLY

Witness names redacted per Emerald Magistrate seal.

**Incident class:** administrative. No body. No numbered case file after closure.

**Subject:** Kitsu Kaoru, Emerald Magistrate, Ryoko Owari quarter.

**Period:** three mornings prior to the interclan renewal spike that would send a lost Crane down his corridor.

**Witness A** ████████ (tea clerk): left cups at the inner desk. Subject did not drink.

**Witness B** ████████ (runner, age twelve): delivered summons at 04:17, 04:22, and 04:31. Subject signed without looking up.

**Witness C** ████████ (puppeteer, street): performed a villain's motive incorrectly. Subject corrected from the magistrate window. No arrest.

**Witness D** ████████ (ronin-adjacent): escorted per House of Falling Lanterns arithmetic. Subject ordered a door closed.

**Finding:** subject maintained ledger discipline. Emotional display: none recorded.

**Recommendation:** file under patronage appetite, spendable witnesses, proprietary corridor. Open the next docket when the renewal petition arrives.

---

The summary sat on cedar while rain taught the eaves to sound like clerks. Kaoru read it once, the way he read confessions: for what was missing. No names worth keeping. No body worth washing. Only the shape of a week in which Ryoko Owari had tried, politely, to offer him warmth in cups he refused to touch.

Bureaucracy had taught him this voice. SUMMARY ONLY. Witness names blacked. Findings that sounded like absolution and functioned like threat. The visual novel the city would later become for one lost Crane already existed here, in docket form: a sequence of doors, a sequence of cups, a sequence of almost-smiles filed under *denied*.

He set the page under his seal and let the first morning begin.

---

### Three mornings

The magistrate wing kept time by rain weight and cup count, not by bells.

On the first morning one cup waited at the corner of his desk, steam long dead. Outside, the canal carried its wrong-green smell up through the palace eaves, the scent of a city that lied about water the way clerks lied about dates. Kaoru read a drowning report and did not touch the tea. The watch had filed another body where the ledger line stayed blank. He underlined the clerk's error in red and set the report on the tray that would become Case One's spine once a dancer knocked and made the numbers personal.

"Tea is witness work when someone wants a favor," he told the empty office, and later, when the tea clerk lingered one breath too long in the doorway, hoping he would sip and soften, he signed her overtime chit instead. "File the cup. I did not ask."

Gratitude in liquid form was still a bid. He did not accept bids before breakfast. The clerk left with coin and without the small victory of having warmed him. Kaoru counted that as kindness, if kindness was defined as not extracting hope he did not intend to honor.

The second morning brought heavier rain and two cups, both empty, lined up like failed petitions. Water streaked the window until the canal road was only lantern smears and the shape of a city that charged rent on visibility. A junior clerk had added sugar to the second cup without being asked, as if sweetness could substitute for a corridor pass.

"You may leave the second if it saves you a walk," he said. "I still will not drink gratitude. If you want to please me, bring dry copies and leave the sugar on someone else's tray."

By noon the sugar had dissolved unused. He noticed and did not notice, the way he noticed witnesses until they stopped being useful.

The third morning there were three cups.

The ledger line for interclan renewal stayed blank one more day. On the outer desk a renewal spike notice waited unopened, edges curled from the humidity. Somewhere in the noble quarter a Crane without local ink was still on the road, turning in a corridor that refused to end. Kaoru knew the type before the knock: permit book, hanko case, spine trained for performance, pride filed under the wrong heading. He had already drafted sponsorship language in his head, donor not danna, because the Crane courts read one line and the canal quarter read an obi on his arm and only he would know both led to the same locked screen.

"Three mornings, three cups, zero admissions," he murmured, and felt his mouth consider something that was not quite a smile. "When the dancer arrives with tea she will learn the same arithmetic. Mikan comes later. Peel on a tray. Domestic filing. I will deny that too until the quarter stops selling what I intend to keep."

The motion filed under *denied*, as usual. In some future afternoon off the ledger, wool and citrus would ask again. He intended to deny it until the city stopped counting his mercies.

---

### Almost-smile inventory

He kept inventories the way other men kept poetry. This one lived on a scrap beside the unused cups.

Almost-smile inventory, magistrate wing, prior week. All entries denied.

When the archive boy brought mikan peel on a tray. Denied.

When Witness ████ cried thank-you on the stairs and bowed too low. Denied.

When the puppet villain on the canal road got the children to clap. Denied.

When the rain stopped for six breaths and the office went quiet enough to pretend this city was kind. Denied.

When the night clerk laughed at his stamp and called him handsome in a voice that wanted a corridor pass. Denied.

When the okami's runner bowed with coin already counted and called him merciful. Denied. Merciful was a word witnesses used before they learned the fee schedule.

When he thought of a kotatsu and wool and citrus and a mouth that might ask him to almost smile on purpose. Denied twice. Denied in advance.

He spoke the inventory aloud because the office was empty and because speaking kept the entries from pretending they were feelings. Proprietary hunger was not romance. It was the appetite to own a corridor, a witness schedule, a body that signed where he pointed. He wrote the distinction on the scrap and tucked it with the seal.

Paper first. Mouth later. If later ever came.

He could have watched the rain another minute. The city looked almost honest when the downpour blurred forgery and festival ash into the same grey. He watched anyway, without smiling. Watching was free. Smiling cost a line item he did not owe.

---

### The runner (not your magistrate)

The boy was twelve and paid per stair. His name is not for this file.

At 04:17 he knelt at the inner screen with a summons packet, third copy, wax still warm from the night clerk. Magistrate wing. Do not knock like a petitioner who wants theater. Slide the paper under the gap.

Through the crack he saw legs crossed on tatami, maroon hem, ink-stained fingers holding a seal. Grey eyes lifted once. Not to the boy. To the docket line where the signature belonged.

The screen opened two fingers wide. A hand took the summons. Stamp. The hand did not pat his head or say thank you.

From inside, a voice the boy would not quote to friends because friends disappeared in this quarter: "Next stair. Do not wait for praise."

At 04:22 the second packet came. Same crack. Same eyes on the line. Same stamp like a heartbeat the boy learned to fear and need.

"If you linger I will deduct a coin for theater."

At 04:31 his knees hurt. Through the gap he saw the magistrate pour a cup of water and leave it untouched beside three tea cups that were never his. The boy understood, without words, that the man inside drank nothing offered and took everything that signed.

"Run."

The screen shut. The boy counted coin on the archive stairs, three deliveries, three stamps, three lessons in how power did not need to look up to take what it wanted. The magistrate never spoke to the corridor. He never speaks to you.

Kaoru returned to his desk and poured water he would not drink, setting the cup beside the tea he had rejected. Witnesses offered liquids. He offered signatures. That was the trade Ryoko Owari understood best.

---

### Play within the office

Midday. Rain thinned to mist until the canal road became a stage the city forgot to charge admission for. From the magistrate window the puppet show looked small and honest, which meant it was lying.

A villain in Scorpion purple strutted between two paper lanterns. A merchant in Crane white wrung his cloth hands. The puppeteer sold a love plot with extra flourishes because flourishes kept children paying attention and parents paying coin. The villain confessed greed. The children booed on cue, delighted to know whom they were allowed to hate.

Kaoru watched from the sill with a cup he still would not drink. His reflection in the glass looked bored. Boredom was useful. It made petitioners perform harder.

"Wrong motive," he said to the window. "He killed for debt schedule, not passion. Ledger line due on the fifteenth. Wife already sold the comb."

Down on the stones the puppeteer made his villain weep for love. Kaoru felt the almost-smile inventory flutter in his chest like a page wanting a stamp.

"Puppeteer, your villain emotes well and files poorly. Fear is cheaper than love in this quarter. Write that on your crate."

The puppeteer bowed to the empty window, not knowing who had corrected him. Kaoru did not applaud. Applause was for dancers who earned it with feet, not with strings.

Patronage appetite sat in his chest like a second seal. He watched the puppet merchant die for love and knew the city sold a prettier lie because pretty lies kept the quarter spending. Sponsorship language lived in the same drawer as custody chits. Donor, not danna. Gifts for artistic expression, interpret liberally. He had not met the Crane yet and already knew he would mean every word of the line and none of the innocence.

"Dancers make the same mistake," he said. "Pretty feet, wrong reason. I will know the difference when one knocks."

He considered sending a clerk to cite the crate for obstruction. Citations kept puppeteers honest and children moving. Instead he let the show end. Street theater was not his docket unless it blocked his corridor. He filed the puppeteer under *nuisance, useful* and returned to the renewal notice he still had not opened.

"When the renewal petition comes I will not be the villain in purple. I will be the stamp. That is scarier and more honest."

---

### Bad-end mirror

Night. Same rain geometry the quarter used when it sold what the ledger would not keep. The bad-end map, if you were cruel enough to draw it, began here: side alley between two shuttered tea houses, dry enough to stand, narrow enough to trap, canal breathing wrong-green at the mouth.

Kaoru walked without escort because escort was a fiction magistrates told when they wanted the watch to look busy. He knew this alley from a brothel game over he had never played as a player, only as a man who signed the arithmetic that made such endings possible. A soaked Crane girl without his seal would meet ronin here. Pretty hair. Wet obi. No chop. House of Falling Lanterns coin. He had read the reports. He had written some of them.

Tonight the alley held a woman who was not that girl and would never be her on the page. She had testified last week on a forgery line. Useful mouth. Spendable now that the factor paid the other side.

"Wrong quarter to be visible without my seal," Kaoru said.

She knew his voice. Witnesses always knew the stamp that had used them. She reached for his sleeve with fingers that had held a brush and signed someone else's guilt. He stepped back into the rain where ink belonged.

"I could open the watch door. I could write you a corridor pass until dawn."

The words were true. He could have. He tasted them like cold tea, offered and refused in the same breath.

"Instead I will close the other door."

Ronin at the alley lip watched without surprise. They had been waiting for a fee line. He signed the chit they expected. Not her name. A number. The okami's arithmetic completed without him watching the cart roll toward the House of Falling Lanterns. The alley door shut on someone else's fate.

*His side*

Harsh mercy was still mercy in the ledger if you did not look at the cart lane.

He could have opened the watch door. He chose the fee line because the factor's payment had already cleared and the witness had served her purpose on the forgery tray. That was the aftercare of power in Ryoko Owari: dry sleeves, correct forms, the body removed from his jurisdiction before it could ask for something he would have to call kindness.

Witness spendable when the quarter paid better. Patronage was appetite with a stamp. He knew what he was buying. Not her. Not tonight. The clean continuation of a week in which his corridor stayed his and his hands stayed ink-stained instead of blood-stained.

You wanted my corridor, he thought, listening to the cart fade. This is what my corridor costs when I do not lend it.

Do not call that romance. Call it jurisdiction.

He felt dread anyway. Dread was a clerk who had already stamped tomorrow. Tomorrow a lost Crane would knock the wrong door and he would call her scheduling. Tomorrow he would drink none of the tea and take all of the witness. Tomorrow he would almost smile and deny it on principle and mean the denial and hate that he meant it.

Aftercare of power did not require looking at the cart. He walked back through rain that did not distinguish brothel geography from magistrate halls. His hands smelled of ink. Good. Men softened. Magistrates stamped.

---

### Case Zero closes

He washed at the basin until the water ran clear. The reflection showed a magistrate, not a man. Good.

In the office he aligned the unused tea cups with the water he had poured for himself. Four vessels. Zero admissions. The redacted summary lay under the seal where clerks would read it and understand nothing about almost-smiles. Case Zero was administrative. No body. No numbered file. Only the prologue's shadow, long before the wrong door opened on a girl with a permit book and a spine trained for performance.

Companion meant my corridor, my hanko, my release, he had written on a blank sheet weeks ago, testing the language. He intended to fill the blank soon.

Renewal season. Wrong door season.

Footsteps in the noble quarter. Lost. Crisp. Obi bells clicking in a corridor that refused to end. He did not rise. Petitioners came to his desk or they did not come at all.

"That is footsteps," he said to the empty room. "Knock if you want something."

Silence, then a knock that was too eager, too Crane, wrong in the way that made his mouth almost move.

"Done complaining yet?"

Denied.

"Wrong door. Again."

He would say it to her face when she came back, because they always came back to the stamp that denied them. Come back when you learn my corridor, he thought, listening to the rain resume. You will.

Case Zero ended where the visual novel docket opened: lantern light on polished floorboards, every sliding door looking the same, and somewhere behind the walls a hunger dressed as law waiting to sign its name on a dancer who did not yet know she was auditioning for his left hand.

---

### The clerk who pasted the summary

Before dawn on the fourth morning a records clerk pasted the redacted summary into the administrative spine and wrote *Case Zero* in the margin where numbered cases would never belong. She read the recommendation line twice.

Patronage appetite. Spendable witnesses. Proprietary corridor.

She had worked the magistrate wing long enough to know those phrases were not accusations. They were job descriptions. She blacked another witness name, aligned the stamp, and went to brew a fourth cup of tea she already knew would die on the desk.

The cup joined the others. The rain joined the rain. Somewhere on the road a Crane turned in a corridor that refused to end, and the city held its breath the way it always did before a stamp decided who got to perform and who got sold.

Case Zero closed. The knock was coming.
